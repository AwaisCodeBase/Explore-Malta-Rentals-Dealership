What this changes for a guest

Nothing underneath has been swapped out. Prices, pickup from Pietà, hotel delivery, the fleet, the languages, WhatsApp, and the booking steps stay. The change is how that offer is presented, from the homepage through the vehicles page, the rates, and the footer.

Less doubt before a booking

The opening second

Before the homepage appears, the name fills the screen. Malta is in the red of the mark, Rentals is in the script of the logo, and a thin route line draws across the dark. A percentage counts up, then the page opens. It is a short, deliberate start, the kind a guest expects from a brand that looks after the details.

The live site opens straight onto the page. The renewal introduces the company first, then the fleet. It plays once, on the way in.

Live site and the renewal

Screens below are the public site as it is today, next to the renewal.

Today the search starts in the same view as the headline, so the photograph never gets a full moment. The renewal gives the opening screen to three bikes in a lit bay, the sign on the wall, and one action: Book now. Pietà stays in the corner. The dark bar and the language control sit in the same frame, which is how an international rental brand holds a page together.

Watch our video stays on this screen. The showroom remains, and the film plays on the screen on the wall, with a close control. The guest can see the ride and still book from the same view. The live homepage has no film on the opening screen.

The search itself is the same useful tool: Pietà or another address, scooter, motorcycle, bicycle or ATV, dates, times, and a clear line that there are no hidden charges. On the renewal it sits on a map of Malta, Gozo and Comino. Choosing a bike reads as planning a route across the islands. The summary under the button still states the length of the trip and the pickup before the guest searches.

Today the two categories sit in matching catalogue cards. They explain the difference, and they look like a template. The renewal shows one bike at a time, in the red and blue of the logo, with a simple switch between 125cc and 50cc. A guest who is not a rider can see the machine and the reason to pick it, then go straight to that part of the fleet.

Same promises on both sides: easy pickup, helmets and phone holders, flexible dates, tourist support, and hotel delivery. Today they sit in a compact grid. In the renewal each promise has its own card, and the icons run through the title so a guest can read the offer before they read the detail.

The live homepage does not have this section. After the fleet it goes straight to services. The renewal puts the path in between: choose the bike, set pickup and return, then ride. Hotel delivery is named in the second step, so a first-time guest knows what happens after they book.

The line is the same: “Malta is waiting, make it yours.” Today it sits in the centre of an empty dark panel. In the renewal it is set across the scooters and ATVs, under the sign, so the guest sees the fleet at the moment they are asked to start.

The vehicles page is where a serious guest compares engine size, colour and dates. Today it opens as a filter column and a logo bar. The renewal opens with the same map as the homepage and a direct headline: find the ride, compare, lock the dates. Transparent pricing is stated before the form. The search behaviour is familiar. The page now looks like part of the same company as the homepage.

The booking itself stays a clear sequence: rental details, options and delivery, guest information, then review and confirm. A returning guest can still load a booking with the reference and email. What the renewal adds on this page is a setting for the price. Longer hires are shown as a plain offer — 20% from 7 days, 28% from 14, 40% from 21 — on a night scene, with the note that the final figure depends on the vehicle, the season and availability. A longer stay looks like a reason to book more days, which is better for the business than a table left on a blank background.

The footer is where a cautious guest checks that the company is real. Both versions carry the Pietà address, email, WhatsApp, the map, the newsletter, and the legal links. The renewal places the map in the blue and red geometry of the logo and sets the name large along the bottom. It closes like a brand, and it still tells the guest exactly where to go: 42, Triq il-Marina, Pietà.

On a phone

Most guests will open this on a phone, often after they land or from a hotel room. The renewal does not shrink the desktop page. The picture, the type, and the placement change so the same offer stays easy to read and easy to tap.

The wide showroom is the desktop opening. On a phone the opening is the coast toward Valletta, with the scooter large in the frame and the route marks still visible. The headline, Book now, and Watch our video stack in one column with space around each. Pietà stays in reach. Menu and Book now sit in the top bar, so the next action is never buried in a row of small links.

The menu opens as a full list, numbered from Home through Services, with the languages in one row at the bottom. A guest does not pinch or hunt. On the vehicles page the map stays, and the search becomes a single column: pickup, drop-off, dates, times, then Search. The fleet cards keep the photo, the price, and Book now large enough to use with a thumb. The result is the same company as the desktop site, with more room on the smaller screen.

The images, and the job of each one

These scenes were composed for the brand. They are there to show a looked-after fleet and Malta as the trip, on desktop and on a phone. They are brand pictures for the website, separate from a photo of the shopfront on Triq il-Marina.

Why this is the right move for the business

Guests arriving from abroad already have a habit of judging a rental company by the site: if it looks assembled, they assume the handover will be too. Explore Malta Rentals is a local operator with a real address, a real fleet, included helmets, hotel delivery where it applies, and someone on WhatsApp. The renewal makes that visible in the order a guest actually looks.

They see the standard of the fleet. They see the islands they came for. They see that 50cc and 125cc are a simple choice. They see the three steps, so the deposit has a story. They see that a longer booking is worth more. They see Pietà on a map before they leave. That sequence is what turns a cautious comparison into a booking with a smaller company, instead of a reflex click toward a larger international name.

The site already serves an international guest list through its languages. The renewal gives that audience a single, consistent face: dark navigation, one orange action, the same map on the pages where people decide, and a footer that still proves the company is in Pietà.

The practical offer is unchanged, which is the point. A guest who books does not have to learn a new process. You do not have to relearn the operation. The public site finally looks like the company you already run.
